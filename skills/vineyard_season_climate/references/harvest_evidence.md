# Evidence requirements for phenology and harvest timing

## What the board may infer

Thermal-forcing models can estimate budbreak, flowering, veraison, or maturity
when stage definitions, cultivar parameters, and validation error are known.
The board may use published cultivar results as candidate priors and compare
them with its local seasonal climate series.

## What the board must measure locally

An operational harvest-window model requires, at minimum:

- dated phenological observations using a declared scale;
- serial Brix or total soluble solids measurements near ripening;
- titratable acidity and pH from the same sampling dates;
- berry weight and crop-load or yield context;
- harvest date and intended wine style;
- preferably vine water status, canopy/exposure context, and multiple vintages.

Every operational model must report its cultivar and field scope, training
sample count, temporal validation design, held-out error in days, composition
error where applicable, and the maturity target it predicts.

## Seed literature for internal comparison

- Piña-Rey et al. validated cultivar-level thermal models for budbreak and
  flowering in north-west Spain: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7998222/>.
- Reshef et al. measured cultivar, site, and season interactions in phenology
  and berry primary metabolism: <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7774500/>.
- Rienth et al. review how temperature, water availability, and solar radiation
  modify berry primary and secondary metabolism:
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8020818/>.
- Caffarra et al. provide a long-term cultivar-by-climate dataset joining
  harvest date with Brix, acidity, pH, and wine traits:
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9170713/>.
- The European phenology study describes calibrated thermal models for
  budbreak, flowering, veraison, and maturity and illustrates why parameter
  availability constrains cultivar transfer:
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10100336/>.

These sources establish variables and candidate model structures. They do not
validate a harvest date for a board field without local observations.
